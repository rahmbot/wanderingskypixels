Night Sky Post Studio

Reads your photo, asks what story you want to tell, and builds an Instagram-ready edit with your watermark kept clear of the hero.

About this project

Most of my editing for Instagram is the same routine. Warm up the moon, pull back the highlights, lift the city out of the shadows, crop to 4:5, and drop the watermark somewhere it won't fight the shot. Filters don't get it right, because every frame needs something a little different.

So I built an editor that reads the photo first, asks what I'm going for, and only then sets the sliders. I can still adjust every one of them. The watermark goes on last, on its own layer, and lands in the quietest corner it can find.

The Problem
One-tap filters treat every photo the same. A moonrise over a skyline needs different help than a bright drone landscape, and the wrong crop or watermark spot can ruin the hero.
The Approach
Measure the frame, offer three distinct directions, ask two or three questions about mood and placement, then blend all of that into a starting edit you can fine-tune.
The Rule
Never edit blind. Nothing gets applied until a direction is picked, and exports only ever come out at 1:1, 4:5 or 16:9 so Instagram never crops them on upload.
How an edit flows
1 · Assess & vibe

Reads exposure, crushed shadows, clipping and color cast. Suggests three looks and asks what you want people to feel.

2 · Edit blueprint

Your answers become real slider values with a note on why. Pick the crop, drag to frame it, and hold to compare with the raw shot.

3 · Watermark & export

The mark is drawn after the grade so it stays crisp, placed away from the hero, then saved as a full-size JPG at 95% quality.

Design Rationale
Ask Before Editing

The questions are the feature

The same moonrise can be calm and warm or dark and dramatic. Both are right, depending on the post. So the tool asks first. Three short questions turn into specific choices, like more grain for a film look or a deeper vignette for drama.

House Style Built In

Moon shots stay warm

My own rules are part of the logic. Moon and sunset frames lean warmer, highlights get pulled before exposure goes up, and crushed shadows get an extra lift so the city reads. It starts where I would start.

Protect The Hero

A watermark that knows where not to go

Auto placement scores each corner for brightness and detail, then picks the darkest, quietest one. The mark is drawn after all the pixel work, so the grade never softens or tints it.

Still Yours

Suggestions, not decisions

Every value the tool picks stays on a slider with a reset. Claude can write the directions and the blueprint, but you always see the reasoning and can change any of it before export.

What's Next

Next up is video. The plan is a reel builder for moon timelapses that follows the style I like most: open on a behind-the-scenes snapshot, cut to the timelapse zoomed out, push in during the hero moment, slow down for the pose, and ease back out. After that, saved presets per series and batch edits for a full shoot.
