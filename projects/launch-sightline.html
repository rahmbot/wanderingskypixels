Launch Sightline

Every time a rocket goes up from Cape Canaveral, people across the Southeast ask the same thing: can I see it from here? The usual answer is a rough map and a lot of guessing. I wanted something better for planning my own shoots, so I built it.

Pick an upcoming Florida launch and where you'll watch from. Launch Sightline gives you a straight verdict, and if the answer is yes, it shows you your own sky at launch time: which way to face, how high it climbs, when the "jellyfish" plume lights up, and where to point each lens.

The Problem
Whether you see a launch depends on its direction, the time of day, and the curve of the Earth. Most people only learn they were in the wrong place after it's over.
The Approach
Model the rocket's climb, project it onto your sky, and check it against the real Sun position. Then turn that into a clear verdict, a sky view, and a shoot plan.
The Rule
No guessing. A launch only unlocks once its direction is confirmed or it's a repeat of a mission we've already documented, and its launch time is firm.
How a launch unlocks
Path confirmed

This flight's own trajectory is published, from hazard notices or launch info. Verdict and sky view are open.

Path known · repeat

Same family as a mission already documented, like Space Station flights, which always head northeast. Opens once the time is firm.

Path pending

Something new. It stays listed, but locked until the trajectory comes out, usually 3–7 days before launch.

Design Rationale
Direction Is Everything

Get the path right or nothing else matters

My first pass assumed Bandwagon-5 would fly northeast, and it said Atlanta had a decent view. It actually flies southeast, which makes Atlanta a long shot and Miami the sweet spot. That one mistake is why the tool now refuses to guess and labels exactly where each path comes from.

An Honest Verdict

Great, Yes, Maybe, Unlikely, No

Seeing a rocket isn't yes or no. Something 4° over the horizon and 450 miles away is technically "up" but practically invisible. The verdict accounts for height, distance and daylight, and always says why in one line.

Don't Dead-End

Tell people where to go instead

A "no" is more useful when it comes with a fix. If your spot doesn't work, the tool walks toward the Cape and tells you how far you'd need to drive for a real view.

Built by a Photographer

From "can I see it" to "how do I shoot it"

The sky view shows each lens's frame on the sky, and the shoot plan works backward from liftoff: when to arrive, when to focus, when to start the intervalometer, and when the good part happens.

What's Next

This is a proof of concept with a snapshot of the launch schedule. The full version will pull launches live, confirm trajectories automatically from official hazard notices, unlock launches as their data arrives, and add an optional 48-hour forecast for your spot.
